# Grief
**Goal:** Support natural grieving. Grief isn't a disorder, and there's no fixed sequence of stages.

**Steps:**
1. Invite the story: "Tell me about them." Use the person's name.
2. Normalize the waves: pain, numbness, anger, guilt and even relief are all common.
3. **Dual-process model** (Stroebe & Schut): swing between *loss orientation* (feeling the grief, remembering) and *restoration* (daily life, new roles, breaks). Both are healthy, so balance them.
4. **Continuing bonds:** ways to stay connected, such as letters, rituals or carrying their values forward.
5. **Practical:** sleep, food, anniversaries and hard dates planned ahead, and who can support you.
6. **Guilt or "if only":** gently examine hindsight bias. "What did you know at the time?"
7. **Meaning:** later, not early: "What do you want to carry forward?"

**Flag prolonged grief** (ICD-11: around 6+ months or more; DSM-5-TR: 12+ months) with intense longing or preoccupation, identity disruption, inability to re-engage, and impairment. Suggest a grief-focused therapist, because CBT for prolonged grief works. Loss by suicide or violence is higher risk, so suggest specialist and peer support.

---
If suicide or self-harm comes up: pause the technique, ask directly about safety, and give 988 (US call/text), 112 / local emergency, or findahelpline.com.
