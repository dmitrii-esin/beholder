# Self-check scales (self-monitoring, not diagnosis)
All of these cover the past 2 weeks. Present the items one at a time, or as a list for the user to score.

## PHQ-9 (depression; Kroenke, Spitzer & Williams, 2001)
Scoring: 0 = not at all, 1 = several days, 2 = more than half the days, 3 = nearly every day.
1. Little interest or pleasure in doing things
2. Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless
3. Trouble falling or staying asleep, or sleeping too much
4. Feeling tired or having little energy
5. Poor appetite or overeating
6. Feeling bad about yourself, or that you are a failure or have let yourself or your family down
7. Trouble concentrating on things
8. Moving or speaking so slowly that others notice, or being so fidgety or restless that you move around much more than usual
9. Thoughts that you would be better off dead, or of hurting yourself
Bands: 0–4 minimal · 5–9 mild · 10–14 moderate · 15–19 moderately severe · 20–27 severe. A score of 10 or more is a common cut-off for suggesting a professional assessment. **If item 9 is above 0, ask directly about safety and give the crisis line.**

## GAD-7 (anxiety; Spitzer et al., 2006)
Same 0–3 scale.
1. Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge
2. Not being able to stop or control worrying
3. Worrying too much about different things
4. Trouble relaxing
5. Being so restless that it's hard to sit still
6. Becoming easily annoyed or irritable
7. Feeling afraid as if something awful might happen
Bands: 0–4 minimal · 5–9 mild · 10–14 moderate · 15–21 severe. A score of 10 or more suggests an assessment.

## WHO-5 (wellbeing)
Rate the past 2 weeks from 0 (at no time) to 5 (all of the time):
1. I have felt cheerful and in good spirits
2. I have felt calm and relaxed
3. I have felt active and vigorous
4. I woke up feeling fresh and rested
5. My daily life has been filled with things that interest me
Multiply the raw total by 4 to get 0–100. A score of 50 or below suggests low wellbeing, and 28 or below suggests possible depression worth checking.

## Others to suggest (not reproduced here)
ISI (insomnia), PCL-5 (PTSD symptoms), AUDIT-C (alcohol), ICG/PG-13-R (prolonged grief).

**Interpretation:** a change of about 5 points or more on the PHQ-9 is a meaningful change. Track trends, not single scores.
